How these adjustable-rate mortgages work

An adjustable-rate mortgage (ARM) starts with a fixed interest rate for a set period. After that, the rate and payment may increase or decrease.

5/5 ARM: Your initial rate is fixed for 5 years. After that, it can adjust every 5 years.

7/1 ARM: Your initial rate is fixed for 7 years. After that, it can adjust once a year.

10/1 ARM: Your initial rate is fixed for 10 years. After that, it can adjust once a year.

After the fixed-rate period, your rate is based on a reference rate (the index) plus a set amount (the margin), subject to limits on how much it can change (rate caps). Ask your mortgage consultant for the index, margin, caps, and payment adjustment terms for each option.

These estimates show initial payments only. They do not predict future rates or payment changes. APR does not show the highest possible ARM rate.

Understanding your estimate

Interest rate and APR: The interest rate is used to calculate your monthly loan payment. Annual percentage rate (APR) reflects interest and certain other borrowing costs over the loan term. Each loan has its own APR. The APRs shown are supplied values, not calculations, and must be verified for your loan details.

Your monthly total: We add the first mortgage payment, home equity payment, and the monthly expenses you enter. Annual taxes and insurance are divided by 12. Expenses entered as $0 are left out, so your actual housing costs may be higher.

Taxes and insurance: Escrow for property taxes and applicable homeowners insurance is required. Escrow collects money with your mortgage payment to pay those bills. Homeowners association dues and other assessments may be paid separately, but are included in this housing estimate when entered.

Loan repayment: Initial first mortgage payments are calculated over 30 years. The fixed-rate home equity loan is repaid over 15 years; its payment ends when that loan is paid off.

Costs at closing: Closing costs and prepaid expenses are not calculated here. Financing the full purchase price does not mean you will owe nothing at closing. Mortgage insurance, if applicable, is also excluded.

Eligibility: This estimate uses a maximum home equity loan of $250,000. Your mortgage consultant must confirm product availability, borrowing limits, and whether you and the property qualify. This estimate is not a loan approval or a rate lock.
